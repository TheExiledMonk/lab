# PBUF STATE-001 — Uniqueness of the universal state

## Decision

**Outcome D, with Outcome C describing the admissible family.** Current PBUF foundations do not imply that exactly one global state is physically realized. They conditionally support a history represented by ordered, complete states of a three-dimensional medium, but the representation does not determine the ontological status of states elsewhere in that order.

Three positions remain internally consistent with the current record:

1. **single-actual-state ontology:** exactly one state is actual; past and future states are non-actual members of a lawful or reconstructed history;
2. **growing-history ontology:** the current and earlier states are actual, while later states are not; and
3. **whole-history ontology:** every state in the history is equally part of the physical structure, although not simultaneous in the effective metric.

The exact missing principle is a **global actuality-and-slicing principle**:

> State whether physical realization applies to one state, an accumulated segment, or the complete ordered history; define the invariant criterion selecting any privileged “current” state; and show that the associated global state/slicing either is gauge or reproduces V11's relativistic observables without an observable preferred foliation.

This is distinct from TIME-001's missing relational temporal-identification principle. TIME-001 asks how order becomes physical duration and proper time. STATE-001 asks what in that order is physically realized. Neither question is answered by V11's effective relativistic calculations.

## 1. Authority and logical boundary

V11 remains authoritative and retains Special Relativity, General Relativity, operational Lorentz invariance, Einstein's equations, and standard quantum dynamics. It uses an effective relativistic spacetime and cosmological evolution but makes no microscopic claim that only one universal state exists. No V11 quantity selects a universal present.

TIME-001 conditionally represents a history as an oriented curve through a physical state space,

`gamma: I -> Q_phys`, `lambda |-> X(lambda)`,

with strictly increasing reparametrizations treated as the same history. This is a representational architecture, not an actuality rule. STATE-001 neither changes it nor assumes that it has already been physically derived.

## 2. Four different meanings of uniqueness

“The universe has a unique state” is ambiguous. Four claims must be separated:

1. **Identity uniqueness:** at a specified history position, `gamma(lambda)` denotes one physical equivalence class rather than several coordinate descriptions. This follows from using a single-valued history representation.
2. **Descriptive uniqueness:** all gauge-related field configurations represent the same physical state. This requires a defined gauge quotient, which PBUF does not yet supply completely.
3. **Dynamical uniqueness:** one complete state has exactly one allowed continuation and predecessor. This requires deterministic, well-posed, invertible dynamics and does not follow from ontology.
4. **Actuality uniqueness:** exactly one member of the history is physically realized. This is the single-actual-state thesis and requires the missing actuality principle.

Only the first is immediate within TIME-001's mathematical representation. It cannot establish the other three.

## 3. What constitutes a complete state?

The strongest current definition is inherited from TIME-001:

`X = [q, p, ..., boundary/global data] in Q_phys`.

It is an equivalence class of sufficient Cauchy data on a three-dimensional carrier `Sigma`, modulo spatial-coordinate and internal gauge redundancies. “Complete” means sufficient to evaluate every physical observable assigned to that instant and, once dynamics exists, to determine the permitted continuations or their probabilities. It does not mean that an observer can know or access all entries.

The exact field content remains unresolved. Depending on the future kinematic and dynamical choice, completeness may require:

- medium configuration variables;
- conjugate or velocity data, or other first-order completion;
- constraints and gauge-equivalence information;
- matter and radiation state, not merely the medium's coarse deformation;
- correlations, memory, or statistical data if the effective description is non-Markovian;
- boundary or asymptotic data where physically required; and
- the clock/ruler data needed for the medium-to-metric identification.

A scalar `u`, a three-component historical lattice variable, a three-metric, or a matter density alone is not established as the complete universal state. Nor does V11's homogeneous scale factor `a` specify a local universal state.

### Global-state existence

A global instantaneous state is stronger than a local state. In the effective relativistic description it normally requires an admissible spacelike Cauchy hypersurface and sufficient global hyperbolicity. Even when such slices exist, many foliations may represent the same four-geometry. Current PBUF does not derive a preferred foliation or prove that every physically allowed solution possesses a global state of this kind.

Therefore the phrase “at an emergent instant” can be used safely only conditionally: relative to an admissible state slicing, or after a new invariant global-state principle is supplied. It cannot silently introduce absolute simultaneity.

## 4. State history versus coordinate history

A **state history** is an ordered curve of physical equivalence classes in `Q_phys`. Its invariant content, if established, consists of the order, physical correlations, and any relational clock readings. A **coordinate history** is a collection of component fields written as functions of a coordinate time or slicing label. Multiple coordinate histories can represent the same physical state history.

Likewise, an effective **spacetime history** is the four-dimensional Lorentzian representation reconstructed from the medium history. It contains events, causal relations, and metric intervals; it is not an additional store through which a three-dimensional substance literally moves. Under a single-state ontology it represents relations among realized and non-realized configurations. Under a whole-history ontology it may represent the complete physical history. The mathematics alone does not choose between those readings.

## 5. Past and future

If the single-actual-state principle is added, then past configurations are earlier members of the ordered history, not physically coexisting objects. A past record is information encoded in the current state. A reconstructed past is a state inferred by applying an inverse dynamical or inferential map to current records and boundary assumptions.

Without that added principle, the conclusion does not follow. Under a whole-history ontology, past states are parts of the physical history without being colocated or simultaneous with the current state. Under a growing-history ontology, earlier states have a different actuality status from later ones. None of these alternatives requires treating time as a material dimension, and none is selected by the mere existence of an ordering.

“Coexistence” must therefore be used carefully. Coexistence on one state means simultaneous inclusion in the same complete state. Existence as another ordered state is a distinct metaphysical relation. Calling both “coexistence” would prejudge the outcome.

The future is likewise not fixed by ontology. It may be one lawful continuation, a branching set of allowed continuations, a probability distribution, an already included portion of a whole history, or not physically actual. These options depend on actuality and dynamics.

## 6. Reconstruction is not motion through time

Reconstruction of a previous state is an operation on present information:

- choose the current equivalence class and records available in it;
- apply a dynamical inverse, constraint solution, or inference rule; and
- obtain one or more candidate earlier equivalence classes.

No physical system is thereby displaced along an additional dimension. The reconstructing apparatus remains part of the current state, and the output is a representation or newly prepared configuration, not identity with the earlier universal state.

Exact reconstruction requires complete data and an invertible law. Approximate reconstruction can be non-unique because of coarse graining, missing records, stochasticity, gauge redundancy, or causal inaccessibility. These are epistemic or dynamical limitations; they do not establish which states are actual.

## 7. Temporal reversal under emergent time

Emergent time changes the interpretation of reversal but not the logical tests for it.

- **Parameter reversal** `lambda -> -lambda` merely reverses notation unless orientation has physical content.
- **History reversal** reverses the ordered sequence and applies the required reversal operation to state data, such as momentum-like quantities. Whether the reversed sequence is admissible is a property of dynamics.
- **State reconstruction** infers an earlier state and is not reversal of the universe's evolution.
- **Preparing a similar configuration** creates a later state with matching selected observables; it does not make it the numerically identical earlier universal state because its global records and ordering relations differ.

The ontology alone supplies no time-reversal symmetry, arrow of time, invertibility, or return law. A primitive orientation may distinguish earlier from later without making the dynamics irreversible. Conversely, symmetric laws can coexist with asymmetric boundary conditions or coarse-grained arrows.

## 8. Ontology versus dynamics

### Direct or definitional consequences

Conditional on the TIME-001 architecture:

- a state is distinct from its label and coordinate representation;
- one history position maps to one state equivalence class in a single-valued history;
- past and future labels are relational positions, not extra material locations;
- relabeling the history does not change its physical content; and
- reconstructing a state is conceptually different from traversing a fourth material dimension.

### Conclusions requiring an actuality principle

- only one state is physically realized;
- earlier states no longer exist, or later states do not yet exist;
- a unique global “now” exists; and
- records are the sole physical status of the past.

### Conclusions requiring dynamics or boundary conditions

- deterministic or probabilistic continuation;
- uniqueness of the future or predecessor;
- reversibility and time-reversal symmetry;
- an arrow of time;
- reconstructibility of past states;
- branching, memory loss, entropy growth, and causal propagation; and
- stability and well-posedness of the state history.

## 9. Mathematical conveniences versus physical claims

The following are conveniences unless independently promoted and justified:

- a real parameter `lambda` labeling history order;
- a chosen foliation `Sigma_lambda`;
- coordinates and field components on each slice;
- lapse/shift-like bookkeeping linking coordinate slices;
- representing the entire history in one diagram or four-manifold; and
- selecting one reconstructed representative from a gauge class.

The following would be physical claims:

- `lambda` is observable absolute time;
- one foliation is the medium's physically preferred simultaneity;
- exactly one state is actual;
- the full curve is physically real;
- the evolution is deterministic, invertible, or irreversible; and
- reconstructed configurations are literally the earlier universe.

No current PBUF authority establishes those physical claims.

## 10. Preservation of the V11 limit

Any future universal-state ontology must preserve V11's effective relativistic regime. At minimum:

1. all observables must be invariant under admissible coordinate changes and equivalent slicings;
2. no observable preferred foliation may contradict operational Lorentz invariance;
3. the one effective Lorentzian metric must govern clocks, light, causal order, and standard matter coupling;
4. local proper time must not be replaced by an unsupported universal clock;
5. the effective initial-value description must remain compatible with the retained Einstein/Noether/Bianchi structure; and
6. V11's `a`, `H(a)`, thermal functions, and cosmological equations retain their published roles.

A hidden fundamental succession could be compatible with V11 only if its foliation is gauge or leaves no conflicting observable structure in the effective regime. That compatibility is a requirement, not a result of STATE-001.

## 11. Answers to the research questions

1. **Unique instantaneous universal state:** identity uniqueness follows within a chosen single-valued history, but a unique physically actual global state does not follow. A global instant itself is conditional on suitable slicing/global structure.
2. **Complete state:** a gauge-equivalence class of all sufficient Cauchy data for medium, matter, constraints, correlations, and clock/ruler content on an admissible three-dimensional carrier. Its detailed content is missing.
3. **Past configurations:** they are earlier ordered states in all candidate ontologies. Whether they remain physically real is undecided; non-simultaneity is not the same as nonexistence.
4. **Reconstruction versus motion:** reconstruction computes or represents candidate earlier states using present data and laws; it does not transport the current universe through an additional material dimension.
5. **Ontology versus dynamics:** state/label separation and ordered-history representation are structural. Actuality, unique global now, determinism, reversal, arrow, and reconstructibility require additional principles or dynamics.

## 12. Completion statement

STATE-001 does not find a contradiction in a single-actual-state PBUF ontology, but it does not derive that ontology. The current foundations admit multiple actuality interpretations and do not define a foliation-independent global present. The milestone is complete by identifying the missing global actuality-and-slicing principle and the separate dynamical assumptions that must not be smuggled into it.
