# PBUF CONTINUUM-STATE-001 — Ontological consequences of a physical elastic continuum

## Decision

**Outcome B: continuum mechanics restricts, but does not uniquely determine, the ontology.** A physical elastic continuum requires a single mechanically occupied complete state on any chosen admissible state slice. Each material element must have one current configuration, one deformation state, and one value of every complete internal variable at that state position. Otherwise the stored energy and constitutive response are not single-valued functions of the state.

This is **mechanical state uniqueness**, not the stronger claim that only one member of the continuum's history is physically real. Standard continuum mechanics represents full trajectories, histories, and spacetime solutions while remaining silent about whether past and future states are non-actual, accumulated, or equally part of reality. It therefore favors a state-evolution reading operationally but does not select among STATE-001's single-actual-state, growing-history, and whole-history actuality interpretations.

The exact boundary is:

> Continuum mechanics supplies a single-state occupancy principle—one complete constitutive state per material element on an admissible state slice—but it supplies no cross-history actuality principle deciding which other ordered states physically exist.

To reach a unique single-actual-state ontology, PBUF still needs STATE-001's **global actuality-and-slicing principle**, now constrained to preserve the mechanical single-occupancy rule and V11's relativistic slicing equivalence.

## 1. Authoritative mechanical inputs

V11 is authoritative. It retains operational Lorentz invariance, General Relativity, Einstein's equations, and standard quantum dynamics. Its elastic language is constitutive and its homogeneous elastic sector does not define a local deformation field or a universal present.

The prior continuum milestones narrow the mechanical class:

- CONSTITUTIVE-CLASS-001 favors a covariant, reversible, energy-storing elastic or hyperelastic medium with a stable unloaded state and a linear tangent regime. It does not choose one local, gradient, or causal-nonlocal realization.
- NATURE-001 retains stable recovery, stored energy, causal wave-capable dynamics, finite admissible response, and tensorially adequate deformation. It imports no named material law.
- KINEMATICS-001 defines only a restricted family of relative-deformation frameworks. A current configuration, reference configuration, relative comparison `C`, gauge quotient, and admissible branch are required, but the primitive field and three-versus-four clock/ruler comparison remain unresolved.
- TIME-001 permits an ordered state history without a fourth ontic material dimension but lacks the clock-to-proper-time identification.
- STATE-001 finds multiple actuality ontologies and lacks a global actuality-and-slicing principle.

CONTINUUM-STATE-001 asks what the first three mechanical inputs add to the last two ontological audits.

## 2. The continuum-mechanical state concept

A classical continuum configuration assigns each material element one placement or one covariant configuration value at a specified state position. Deformation is a relation between the current configuration and an appropriate reference or comparison structure. In PBUF's unresolved covariant notation, the mechanically relevant data may be summarized schematically as

`X = [q, C[q,q_0], internal variables, matter data, constraints, ...]`.

This is classification notation, not a new kinematic or constitutive equation. The precise entries remain open under KINEMATICS-001.

For a stored-energy description, the current energy and response must be evaluated from one admissible state (and, where applicable, its gradients, internal variables, or history summary). If the same material element had two incompatible complete placements or deformation tensors at the same mechanical instant, then density, strain invariants, stored energy, and stress would be ambiguous. Such a structure would no longer be one ordinary continuum state unless an additional branch, mixture, ensemble, or internal variable were added.

Once that extra information is added, the result is again one enlarged complete state. Thus mechanical single-state occupancy is robust under richer state spaces.

## 3. Does mechanics allow multiple deformation states at once?

### Not as incompatible complete states of one element

At a fixed admissible state position, one material element cannot simultaneously occupy two mutually incompatible complete deformation states in an ordinary single-continuum description. A single-valued configuration is required to define deformation and objective state functions.

### What may look like multiplicity

Continuum theories nevertheless use several forms of multiplicity:

- **spatial heterogeneity:** different material elements have different local deformation states within one global state;
- **multiphase or mixture fields:** several constituents occupy a region, but constituent labels and fractions are entries of one larger state;
- **microstructure or internal variables:** multiple modes coexist as components of one state;
- **statistical ensembles:** alternative states encode uncertainty or distributions, not simultaneous mechanical occupancy by one realized classical continuum;
- **multiple equilibrium branches:** several states are admissible solutions, but a realized body occupies one branch unless branch fractions/interfaces are included in the state;
- **memory dependence:** response depends on a past segment or sufficient memory variable, but this does not make the earlier configurations current deformation states; and
- **superposed increments or modes:** linear combinations describe one resulting configuration when the theory permits them, not multiple complete universes.

These cases do not establish co-occupancy of incompatible complete universal states.

## 4. Deformation and reference state

Deformation is relational. KINEMATICS-001 requires a current configuration `q`, an unloaded configuration `q_0`, and a relative comparison `C[q,q_0]` with `C=1` in the unloaded state. The reference need not be a physically coexisting earlier universe. It may be a gauge equivalence class, material labeling structure, natural configuration, or counterfactual comparison state.

Accordingly, using both “current” and “reference” configurations in one formula does not imply that the continuum occupies two complete physical states. Mechanics distinguishes the state being occupied from the structure used to measure its deformation. PBUF has not yet decided whether its reference is fixed, instantaneous FLRW, or temperature-dependent, but none of these mathematical choices by itself settles history actuality.

## 5. Stored energy and state uniqueness

A recoverable elastic medium is characterized structurally by energy stored as a state function or action contribution on its admissible configuration. This imposes three ontological restrictions:

1. **Evaluability:** the current complete state must determine a definite energy assignment, possibly after including gradients or internal variables.
2. **Objectivity:** gauge- or coordinate-related descriptions cannot count as different physical states with different energy.
3. **Branch completeness:** if energy has multiple admissible branches, the branch or phase information must be part of the complete state.

Stored energy does not imply that only the current state exists. Energy functions are defined over many possible states, and action or trajectory formulations compare multiple states mathematically. The domain of a state function is a possibility space, not a claim that every possible state is simultaneously occupied. Conversely, representing the entire solution history does not decide whether every state in that solution is physically actual.

## 6. Constitutive response and evolution

Constitutive response maps the mechanically complete local state—and, depending on the admitted class, its gradients, internal variables, or causal memory—to stress or other response quantities. Single-valued response requires sufficient state specification. If identical listed inputs yield different responses because of hidden history, the listed variables were not a complete constitutive state.

Evolution is a succession of complete continuum states. This naturally supports TIME-001's ordered-history representation:

`... -> X_1 -> X_2 -> X_3 -> ...`

The arrows here denote ordering or admissible succession, not a new equation. Continuum mechanics therefore makes emergent time as **ordered state evolution** mechanically natural: observable change is tracked through successive configurations, and no additional material direction is required for the continuum itself.

But mechanics does not derive:

- the invariant duration between states;
- a monotonic relational clock;
- the one Lorentzian metric and proper time;
- a universal state slicing;
- determinism, reversibility, or an arrow of time; or
- the actuality status of states elsewhere in the history.

Those remain TIME-001, GEOMETRY-001, STATE-001, and future dynamical closure problems.

## 7. What ontology follows naturally?

The most natural **mechanical reading** is current-state realism: constitutive quantities describe the state presently occupied on the chosen state slice, while earlier states enter through records, reference comparisons, or memory data and later states enter as admissible continuations. This reading aligns directly with laboratory continuum language such as current configuration, loading, unloading, and evolution.

However, “natural reading” is not “logical consequence.” The same mechanical predictions can be embedded in:

- a **single-actual-state ontology**, where only the occupied current state is actual;
- a **growing-history ontology**, where the current mechanical state is the boundary of an accumulated actual history; or
- a **whole-history ontology**, where each slice has one mechanically occupied state and the complete trajectory is also ontologically real.

Whole-history and growing-history views are mechanically meaningful as interpretations of the same solution structure, but their claims about cross-history existence are mechanically idle unless they alter admissible states, response, causality, or observables. Mechanics neither requires nor excludes them.

## 8. Mechanical realization versus metaphysical actuality

The phrase “physically realized” must be indexed carefully:

- **mechanically realized at a state position:** the configuration whose deformation, energy, and response are being evaluated;
- **ontologically actual across history:** the portion of the ordered history asserted to exist.

Continuum mechanics answers the first. It requires one complete mechanically realized state per element at a state position. It does not answer the second. Inferring single-history actuality from mechanical single-occupancy would equivocate between these meanings.

This also resolves the apparent tension with STATE-001. Continuum mechanics strengthens STATE-001 by excluding simultaneous occupation of incompatible complete states on one state slice. It does not remove STATE-001's three cross-history actuality branches.

## 9. Where continuum theory ends

Continuum theory determines or constrains:

- the need for a configuration and objective deformation measure;
- one complete state per element at a state position;
- state sufficiency for energy and constitutive response;
- inclusion of branch, mixture, memory, or internal data when necessary;
- ordered succession of configurations for evolution; and
- covariance/objectivity of physical state descriptions.

Additional ontology begins with:

- whether only the current state exists;
- whether past states remain actual;
- whether future states already belong to reality;
- whether a global present is fundamental;
- whether the entire trajectory has independent physical existence; and
- whether a fundamental slicing is physical, gauge, or absent.

Additional dynamics begins with:

- which successor states are allowed;
- whether evolution is unique, branching, invertible, dissipative, or stochastic;
- propagation, causal domains, and memory laws; and
- any time-reversal symmetry or thermodynamic arrow.

## 10. V11 relativistic limit

The mechanical single-state principle must be applied on admissible state slices without turning a coordinate slice into an observable universal present. Any completion must:

1. preserve equivalent descriptions under diffeomorphisms and admissible refoliations;
2. recover one Lorentzian metric governing matter, light, causal structure, and local proper time;
3. avoid an observable preferred frame or simultaneity conflicting with V11's operational Lorentz invariance;
4. retain V11's Einstein, Noether/Bianchi, quantum, thermal, and cosmological architecture; and
5. treat V11's background functions as effective history observables, not microscopic state selectors.

In a relativistic continuum, “current state” is therefore conditional on a valid initial-data representation. The invariant physics lies in the equivalence class of descriptions and local causal relations, not in an arbitrary coordinate-time slice.

## 11. Answers to the research questions

1. **One deformation state at an instant:** yes, in the mechanical sense. A complete state assigns one configuration/deformation to each material element on an admissible state slice.
2. **Multiple complete deformation states:** not simultaneously for one ordinary continuum element. Mixtures, branches, ensembles, memory, and microstructure require an enlarged single state or describe alternatives.
3. **Natural ontology:** current-state realism is the natural working interpretation, but mechanics proves only single-state occupancy, not single-state actuality across history.
4. **Whole/growing histories:** mechanically meaningful as interpretations of complete trajectories or solution histories, but mechanically underdetermined and normally prediction-neutral.
5. **Emergent time:** ordered continuum evolution naturally supports it structurally, but physical duration and relativistic proper time still require TIME-001's missing temporal identification.

## 12. Completion statement

Treating spacetime as a real elastic continuum materially narrows the ontology: it rules out simultaneous mechanical occupation of incompatible complete deformation states and requires a single sufficiently specified constitutive state at each admissible state position. It does not decide how much of the ordered history is ontologically actual. The correct result is Outcome B, with the remaining closure supplied only by a global actuality-and-slicing principle compatible with V11.
