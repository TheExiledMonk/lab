# PBUF IDENTITY-001 — Identity of the physical universe and the spacetime medium

## Decision

**Outcome B, with Outcome C at the cross-history boundary: identifying the spacetime medium with the physical universe constrains ontology, but does not uniquely determine it.** The identity premise says that there are not two underlying physical subjects—“the medium” and “the universe”—whose states must be matched. A complete realized state of the medium is a complete realized state of the universe, subject to whatever non-medium sectors V11 includes as state content rather than as a second universe.

At each admissible state position, one physical continuum realizes exactly one complete configuration. This follows from identity together with the already established complete-state and mechanical single-occupancy conditions. It excludes treating two incompatible complete configurations as simultaneously realized states of the same continuum at the same state position. It does **not** follow from identity alone that only one state in the continuum's ordered history is actual.

Identity also distinguishes physical realization from mathematical representation. The admissible-state space, an ensemble, a reference configuration, a reconstructed past, and a set of possible futures can describe the one universe without becoming additional physical universes. A history representation can likewise describe evolution without its mere use deciding whether the whole history is ontically real.

The exact missing principle is:

> **Relativistic actuality principle:** specify, independently of coordinates and arbitrary foliation, whether physical actuality attaches to exactly one complete continuum state, to a growing ordered segment, or to a complete history; define the equivalence conditions under which different admissible slicings represent the same physical actuality; and add no observable preferred simultaneity inconsistent with V11.

IDENTITY-001 therefore strengthens the reality-versus-representation boundary but does not supply the branch-selecting actuality rule.

## 1. Authority and inherited boundary

V11 remains authoritative. The inherited effective description retains operational Lorentz invariance, General Relativity, one effective Lorentzian metric for clocks and causal structure, and standard matter and quantum dynamics. Nothing in this milestone modifies those commitments.

The relevant prior results are:

- KINEMATICS-001 requires current and reference configurations, an objective relative comparison, gauge quotienting, and an admissible branch, but does not uniquely fix the primitive configuration.
- TIME-001 permits an ordered, reparametrization-equivalent curve of states while leaving the relational-clock-to-proper-time identification open.
- STATE-001 leaves single-actual-state, growing-history, and whole-history ontologies compatible and identifies the absence of a global actuality-and-slicing principle.
- CONTINUUM-STATE-001 derives mechanical single-occupancy: one complete constitutive state per material element on an admissible state slice. It explicitly does not derive cross-history actuality.

IDENTITY-001 adds only the premise `spacetime medium = physical universe`. This is an ontological identification, not a field equation, constitutive relation, or definition of a preferred present.

## 2. Identity is not representation

An identity claim concerns the referent: the physical entity called “the spacetime medium” is the physical entity called “the universe.” It removes a two-substance picture in which a medium exists inside, underneath, or alongside a separately constituted universe.

A representation supplies mathematical objects used to describe that referent. Coordinates, fields, state vectors, equivalence classes, configuration spaces, foliations, histories, ensembles, and diagrams may be indispensable representations without being further physical entities numerically distinct from the universe.

Three distinctions follow.

1. **One referent, many descriptions.** Gauge-, coordinate-, and admissibly refoliation-related descriptions may represent one physical state.
2. **One referent, many admissible alternatives.** A theory may assign many possible states to the universe although only the realized alternative at a specified state position is occupied.
3. **One referent, disputed temporal extent.** Identity does not determine whether the referent is an instantaneous state, an accumulating history, or a whole history. That is an additional claim about the mode and extent of actuality.

The word “itself” adds no hidden dynamics. It does not select a state, advance evolution, create a global now, or turn a mathematical history into a physical object.

## 3. Possibility space versus physical realization

Let `Q_phys` denote, purely as classification notation, the set of gauge-equivalence classes of complete admissible continuum states. Identity does not imply that `Q_phys` is itself a physical super-universe or that every member is realized. It says that any member which is physically realized is a state of the universe itself, not a state of a model-medium standing apart from the universe.

The following must remain separate:

- **admissible state:** satisfies the eventual kinematic, constraint, and constitutive requirements;
- **realized state at a state position:** the complete configuration occupied by the physical continuum there;
- **represented state:** a mathematical description which may be actual, counterfactual, reconstructed, or merely possible;
- **history:** an ordered relation or curve among states;
- **possibility space:** the domain of alternatives over which laws, initial data, or inference range.

Admissibility is not actuality. Mathematical membership in a solution set is not realization. Probability weight is not a second occupied configuration. A reference configuration is not automatically a coexisting universe. A reconstructed earlier state is a present representation unless an independent actuality principle assigns the earlier state further ontic status.

## 4. Can one continuum realize multiple complete configurations?

### Same state position

No, not as mutually incompatible complete configurations of one ordinary continuum. If one alleged realization assigns a material element, metric-relevant datum, branch, or complete internal variable one value while another assigns an incompatible value at the same state position, both cannot be the complete state of the numerically same continuum there.

This conclusion has two supports:

- CONTINUUM-STATE-001 supplies mechanical single-occupancy and state completeness.
- Identity transfers that state claim directly to the physical universe: no separate “real universe” remains which could occupy a different complete state behind the medium description.

Apparent counterexamples do not overturn the result. Spatially varying fields, mixtures, phase fractions, correlations, internal modes, uncertainty distributions, quantum state content retained by V11, and branch labels must be included in one sufficiently enlarged complete state. Alternative complete solutions remain alternatives unless an additional ontology says otherwise.

### Different state positions

Identity alone is silent. It is consistent with:

- **single-actual-state ontology:** exactly one state is actual, with past and future represented by records, reconstructions, or possibilities;
- **growing-history ontology:** an ordered actual segment exists, with the current state as its boundary; and
- **whole-history ontology:** the physical universe is the complete relativistic history, with each admissible slice carrying one locally complete state.

These views disagree about temporal actuality, not about same-position single-occupancy. A whole-history view does not say that one element bears incompatible values at the same event or slice; it assigns different values at different ordered locations in the history. Identity supplies no rule declaring that reading physical or merely representational.

## 5. Current state and the admissible-state set

Identity makes the category distinction sharp:

- the **current physical state**, if the chosen ontology contains a current boundary, is the state realized by the universe;
- the **admissible-state set** is the mathematical domain of states the completed theory permits or considers.

The realized state may be represented by an element of that set, but is not identical to the set. Nor does the existence of the set imply that all its elements exist physically. This is a type distinction between a physical referent and a representational domain.

The qualification “if the chosen ontology contains a current boundary” is essential. Identity does not derive a universal current state in relativistic physics. Locally defined present data and Cauchy-state representations may be valid, while different admissible foliations describe the same effective spacetime. Promoting one foliation's “current” slice to absolute actuality requires the missing principle and a V11 compatibility proof.

## 6. Are histories, futures, and possibility spaces physical objects?

From identity alone:

- a **possibility space** is a representation of alternatives, not an additional physical object;
- a **possible future set** describes allowed continuations unless an added ontology reifies some or all continuations;
- a **history representation** describes ordered evolution, but its mathematical completeness does not decide its ontic extent; and
- records, memories, reference data, and reconstructions are content or representations available in a state, not automatically the numerically identical earlier universe.

It would be too strong to conclude that histories can never be physical. Under whole-history ontology, the complete history is the universe's ontic extent rather than a second object in addition to the universe. Under growing-history ontology, an accumulated segment has physical status. Under single-actual-state ontology, only the realized state does. Identity is compatible with all three because it fixes what entity is under discussion, not how temporal actuality is distributed.

Branching or many-history actuality would require an even stronger additional principle specifying whether branches are parts of one universe, distinct universes, or only possibilities. Identity does not supply that taxonomy, and IDENTITY-001 does not adopt it.

## 7. Consequences of identity and their dependencies

### Consequences of identity alone

1. The spacetime medium and physical universe are one referent, not two interacting or coincident substances.
2. A physically realized medium property is a property of the physical universe; there is no independent universe-state behind it.
3. Mathematical descriptions of the medium do not become additional physical universes merely because the medium is real.
4. Any ontological completion must apply to this one referent consistently; it cannot assign incompatible ontologies to “medium” and “universe.”

### Consequences of identity plus inherited continuum-state results

1. At each admissible state position, the universe realizes one sufficiently complete continuum state.
2. The same continuum cannot simultaneously realize two incompatible complete configurations at that same state position.
3. Mixture, branch, memory, quantum, or internal information required for physical sufficiency belongs to one enlarged state rather than constituting hidden co-occupancy.
4. The realized state is categorically distinct from the set of admissible or represented states.

### Not consequences of identity

- exactly one state across the whole history is actual;
- an objective global present or preferred foliation exists;
- past states have ceased to exist or future states do not yet exist;
- the complete history is a physical object;
- every mathematically possible state or future is physically realized;
- evolution is deterministic, branching, reversible, irreversible, or reconstructible;
- time is fundamental or fully emergent; or
- V11's effective four-dimensional spacetime representation is merely unreal.

## 8. Additional assumptions still required

### Ontological closure

The relativistic actuality principle quoted in the Decision must choose the extent of actuality and state how that choice is invariant under admissible descriptions. If it chooses one actual state, it must provide a physical, foliation-independent selector or explain why the selection is gauge-hidden and observationally equivalent. If it chooses a growing or whole history, it must define that history's ontic status without treating a diagram or solution set as proof of existence.

### State individuation

A complete gauge-invariant state and the identity conditions between states must be defined. This includes the treatment of matter, correlations, constraints, branch or memory data, and the one effective metric. Numerical equality of coordinates or field components is neither necessary nor sufficient for physical identity.

### Temporal and dynamical closure

TIME-001's relational temporal-identification principle remains necessary to connect ordered change to V11 proper time. Separate dynamics must determine admissible successors, probabilities if any, causal propagation, and reversibility. None is entailed by ontological identity.

## 9. Compatibility with V11's relativistic description

Identity can be compatible with V11 provided “medium state” is not tied to an arbitrary coordinate slice. Any completion must:

1. identify gauge-, coordinate-, and admissibly refoliation-related representations where they describe the same physical state or history;
2. preserve operational Lorentz invariance and avoid an observable preferred frame or absolute simultaneity;
3. preserve the single effective Lorentzian metric governing clocks, light, causal order, and matter in the V11 regime;
4. leave V11's Einstein/Noether/Bianchi, quantum, thermal, and cosmological architecture unchanged; and
5. distinguish a fundamental ontological claim from the effective four-dimensional representation without denying the latter's physical observables.

A preferred state succession could be admitted only if independently justified and shown to be gauge or operationally invisible where V11 requires relativistic equivalence. Identity itself provides no such justification.

## 10. Answers to the research questions

1. **One configuration at each stage:** yes, for a specified admissible state position, using identity plus complete-state single-occupancy. Identity alone neither defines a “stage” nor selects a global slicing.
2. **Current state versus admissible set:** yes. The former is a physical realization if the ontology admits a current boundary; the latter is a mathematical domain of alternatives. They are different categories.
3. **History, future, and possibility spaces:** their mathematical forms are descriptions. Their ontic status is not fixed by identity. A whole or growing history can be the ontic extent of the one universe only under an added actuality principle.
4. **Identity-only conclusions:** one referent replaces a medium/universe duality, realized medium properties are universe properties, and representations are not thereby reified.
5. **Additional assumptions:** a relativistic actuality principle, gauge-invariant state individuation, TIME-001's clock/proper-time identification, and independent dynamics.

## 11. Completion statement

Identifying the spacetime medium with the physical universe closes the gap between a merely instrumental medium model and the bearer of physical properties. It does not close the gap between same-position state uniqueness and cross-history actuality. The strongest justified conclusion is therefore: **one universe, one complete realized state per admissible state position, many possible representations and admissible alternatives, and no identity-derived decision about the ontic extent of history.**
